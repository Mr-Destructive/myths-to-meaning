## Shiv Tandav Stotram

The [Shiv Tandav Stotram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva_Tandava_Stotra) is a powerful hymn dedicated to Lord Shiva, celebrating his cosmic dance, the Tandava. It is suspected to be composed by Ravana (not proven though), and it expresses awe for Shiva’s divine qualities, strength, and the profound energy of his dance, which symbolizes creation, preservation, and destruction. The storm serves as a prayer for spiritual awakening, invoking blessings of purity, power, and auspiciousness.

There are a total of [17 verses](https://hindi.webdunia.com/aarti-chalisa/shiv-tandav-stotram-114100100059_1.html) in the stotram. We will be exploring each verse separately and understanding its interpretation literally, mythologically as well as spiritually.

However, people might call it a destructive act but as with anything in the world, it depends on how you perceive it. If you take it positively it is a great source of inspiration and a heartful praise sung by the author (whoever it may be).

## Verse 10

> **अखर्वसर्वमंगला कलाकदम्बमंजरी**
>
> **रसप्रवाह माधुरी विजृंभणा मधुव्रतम्‌।**
>
> **स्मरांतकं पुरातकं भावंतकं मखांतकं**
>
> **गजांतकांधकांतकं तमंतकांतकं भजे ॥**
>
> Akharva-Sarva-Mangalaa Kalaa-Kadamba-Manjari
>
> Rasa-Pravaaha Maadhuri Vijrun-Bhanaa Madhu-Vratam
>
> Smarantakam Purantakam Bhavantakam Makhantakam
>
> Gajaanta-Kaandha-Kaanta-Kam Tamanta-Kanta-Kam Bhaje

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Meaning

I worship Lord Shiva, who is eternally auspicious and a collection of all divine qualities and blessings, like a bouquet of flowers, whose nature is an overflowing stream of sweetness and charm, and whose presence is so alluring that devotees, like bees, are drawn to him.

I worship Lord Shiva, who is the destroyer of desire (Kama), the demon cities (Tripura), worldly attachments, and false pride in ritual sacrifices, who defeated Gajasura (the ego), Andhaka (inner darkness), and even conquered death (Yama).

## Word by Word Meaning

> **अखर्वसर्वमंगला कलाकदम्बमंजरी**
>
> **रसप्रवाह माधुरी विजृंभणा मधुव्रतम्‌**

> Akharva-Sarva-Mangalaa Kalaa-Kadamba-Manjari
>
> Rasa-Pravaaha Maadhuri Vijrun-Bhanaa Madhu-Vratam

I worship Lord Shiva, who is eternally auspicious and a collection of all divine qualities and blessings, like a bouquet of flowers, whose nature is an overflowing stream of sweetness and charm, and whose presence is so alluring that devotees, like bees, are drawn to him

- **अखर्वसर्वमंगला (Akharva-Sarva-Mangalaa): One who is eternally auspicious, bestowing blessings upon all.**

  - अखर्व (Akharva): Eternal, unbroken, undiminished

  - सर्वमंगला (Sarva-Mangala): All-auspicious, bestowing blessings to everyone

- **कलाकदम्बमंजरी (Kalaa-Kadamba-Manjari): A bouquet or collection of divine qualities and arts, representing the embodiment of all beautiful and auspicious virtues.**

  - कला (Kala): Art, skill, or divine attribute; also can mean the phases of the moon

  - कदम्ब (Kadamba): Cluster, group, or collection

  - मंजरी (Manjari): Bouquet or collection of flowers

- **रसप्रवाह (Rasapravaaha):** Stream or flow of nectar, essence, or sweetness

- **माधुरी (Maadhuri):** Sweetness, charm

- **विजृंभणा (Vijrumbana): Expansion, expression, or manifestation**

- **मधुव्रतम्‌ (Madhu-Vratam): One who attracts devotees like bees drawn to honey, symbolizing devotion**

  - मधु (Madhu): Honey, nectar, or sweetness

  - व्रतम् (Vratam): Vow or devotion

> **स्मरांतकं पुरातकं भावंतकं मखांतकं**
>
> **गजांतकांधकांतकं तमंतकांतकं भजे**

> Smarantakam Purantakam Bhavantakam Makhantakam
>
> Gajaanta-Kaandha-Kaanta-Kam Tamanta-Kanta-Kam Bhaje

I worship Lord Shiva, who is the destroyer of desire (Kama), the demon cities (Tripura), worldly attachments, and false pride in ritual sacrifices, who defeated Gajasura (the ego), Andhaka (inner darkness), and even conquered death (Yama).

- **स्मरांतकं (Smarantakam): The destroyer of Kama (desire), indicating control over desires and attachments**

  - स्मर (Smara): Refers to Kama, the god of desire

  - अंतकं (Antakam): Destroyer

- **पुरातकं (Purantakam): The destroyer of the three cities (Tripura) of demons, symbolizing the destruction of the ego and illusions**

  - पुर (Pura): City

  - अंतकं (Antakam): Destroyer

- **भावंतकं (Bhavantakam): The one who liberates from worldly attachments and the cycle of rebirth**

  - भाव (Bhava): Worldly existence, the cycle of birth and death (Samsara)

  - अंतकं (Antakam): Destroyer

- **मखांतकं (Makhaatakam): The one who destroys false pride in rituals, symbolizing humility and genuine devotion, and the one who defeated Andhaka, symbolizing the overcoming of ignorance and inner darkness**

  - मख (Makha): Sacrifice, often referring to pride in ritual sacrifice

  - अंतकं (Antakam): Destroyer

- **गजांतकांधकांतकं (Gajaanta-Kaandha-Kaanta-Kam): The one who defeated Yama (death), representing mastery over mortality and fear of death**

  - गज (Gaja): Elephant, referring to Gajasura, a demon with the form of an elephant

  - अंधक (Andhaka): A demon symbolizing darkness and ignorance

  - अंतकं (Antakam): Destroyer.

- **तमंतकांतकं (Tamanta-Kaanta-Kam):**

  - तम (Tama): Darkness, referring to Yama, the god of death

  - अंतकं (Antakam): Destroyer

- **भजे (Bhaje): Worship or revere.**

## Mythological Meaning

The verse portrays Shiva as the eternal source of auspiciousness and divine beauty, whose presence is like a bouquet of blessings, balancing his powers as both creator and destroyer within the cosmos. The verse also emphasizes Shiva’s role as a divine protector who magnetically draws devotees seeking liberation, symbolizing his loving and approachable nature despite his power.

The verse highlights Shiva’s role as the force that removes obstacles to spiritual progress, destroying desire, illusion, and pride. His actions in mythology serve as reminders that spiritual growth requires overcoming attachments and the ego. The verse also illustrates Shiva as the vanquisher of primal ego, ignorance, and even death itself. In mythological tales, these actions show his supreme powers and protection for devotees, freeing them from inner and outer obstacles on the path to spiritual liberation.

## Spiritual Meaning

- **Mastery over Desire and Attachments**: Transcend attachment to fleeting pleasures and cultivate inner peace and detachment, which are essential for spiritual growth.

- **Conquering the Ego and Illusions**: Spiritual liberation comes from the destruction of ego and the realization that the material world is temporary. True freedom arises when we let go of illusions and recognize the eternal nature of the soul.

- **Breaking Free from the Cycle of Birth and Death**: Liberation is not just an external concept but an internal experience of awakening. Breaking free from the cycle of life and death requires spiritual discipline, deep meditation, and surrender to divine will.

- **The Power of Purity in Rituals and Devotion**: True spirituality lies in the intention behind rituals, not in their outward display. Devotion to the Divine must be heartfelt and free from ego.

- **Mastery Over Fear and Death**: True spirituality is not bound by the fear of death. Through Shiva’s grace, we learn that death is not an end, but a transition. Surrendering to divine will allows us to live beyond the limitations of fear and mortality.

- **The Importance of Inner Transformation**: Spiritual awakening is an inner journey. Confronting and transforming your internal weaknesses, illusions, and attachments are essential steps towards enlightenment.

- **Divine Protection and Guidance**: Trust in the Divine’s guidance, knowing that Shiva’s grace will lead you through the trials and tribulations of life. Surrender to his will for divine protection and spiritual growth.
