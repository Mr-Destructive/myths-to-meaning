## Shiv Tandav Stotram

The [Shiv Tandav Stotram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva_Tandava_Stotra) is a powerful hymn dedicated to Lord Shiva, celebrating his cosmic dance, the Tandava. It is suspected to be composed by Ravana (not proven though), and it expresses awe for Shiva’s divine qualities, strength, and the profound energy of his dance, which symbolizes creation, preservation, and destruction. The storm serves as a prayer for spiritual awakening, invoking blessings of purity, power, and auspiciousness.

There are a total of [17 verses](https://hindi.webdunia.com/aarti-chalisa/shiv-tandav-stotram-114100100059_1.html) in the stotram. We will be exploring each verse separately and understanding its interpretation literally, mythologically as well as spiritually.

However, people might call it a destructive act but as with anything in the world, it depends on how you perceive it. If you take it positively it is a great source of inspiration and a heartful praise sung by the author (whoever it may be).

## Verse 4

> **जटाभुजंगपिंगल स्फुरत्फणामणिप्रभा**
>
> **कदंबकुंकुमद्रव प्रलिप्तदिग्व धूमुखे।**
>
> **मदांधसिंधु रस्फुरत्वगुत्तरीयमेदुरे**
>
> **मनोविनोदद्भुतं बिंभर्तुभूत भर्तरि ॥**
>
> Jataa-Bhujanga-Pingala Sphurat-Phana-Mani-Prabhaa
>
> Kadamba-Kum-Ku-Madrava Pralipta-Digva Dhu-Mukhe
>
> Madanda-Sindhu Rasphuratva-Gurtariya-Medure
>
> Mano-Vinoda-Madbhutam Bim-Bharata-Bhuta Bhartari

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Meaning

The shining of the jewels on the hoods of the serpents entwined in Lord Shiva’s matted hair gives off a fiery glow. The fluid from Kadamba flowers and kumkum (vermilion) is smeared on his forehead, causing his face to radiate and glow in all directions.

His body radiates intensely, adorned with garments as shimmering as an intoxicated elephant. He is the astonishing delight of the mind and the bearer of all beings, Lord Shiva.

## Mythological Meaning

The imagery of **serpents in Shiva's hair** and the **fiery glow of their jewels** symbolize his control over the forces of nature, life, and death. Despite his fierce aspects, Shiva is described with radiant beauty, adorned with Kadamba flowers and kumkum (vermilion), representing his divine and cosmic grace. His attire, often described as silken, signifies that Shiva embodies both aesthetic beauty and spiritual purity, showing the harmony of the material and spiritual realms.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Spiritual Meaning

- **Mastery Over Chaos and Fear**: The serpents in Shiva's matted hair symbolize control over fear and inner turmoil. This teaches us to overcome the chaos of our emotions, desires, and attachments, achieving inner peace through self-mastery and detachment.

- **Finding Joy and Liberation Through Devotion**: Shiva is described as the delight of the mind and sustainer of all beings, reminding us that true joy and liberation come through devotion and connection with the divine.
