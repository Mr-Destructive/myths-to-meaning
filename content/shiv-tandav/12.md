## Shiv Tandav Stotram

The [Shiv Tandav Stotram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva_Tandava_Stotra) is a powerful hymn dedicated to Lord Shiva, celebrating his cosmic dance, the Tandava. It is suspected to be composed by Ravana (not proven though), and it expresses awe for Shiva’s divine qualities, strength, and the profound energy of his dance, which symbolizes creation, preservation, and destruction. The storm serves as a prayer for spiritual awakening, invoking blessings of purity, power, and auspiciousness.

There are a total of [17 verses](https://hindi.webdunia.com/aarti-chalisa/shiv-tandav-stotram-114100100059_1.html) in the stotram. We will be exploring each verse separately and understanding its interpretation literally, mythologically as well as spiritually.

However, people might call it a destructive act but as with anything in the world, it depends on how you perceive it. If you take it positively it is a great source of inspiration and a heartful praise sung by the author (whoever it may be).

## Verse 12

> **दृषद्विचित्रतल्पयो र्भुजंगमौक्तिकमस्र**
>
> **जोर्गरिष्ठरत्नलोष्ठयोः सुहृद्विपक्षपक्षयोः।**
>
> **तृणारविंदचक्षुषोः प्रजामहीमहेन्द्रयोः**
>
> **समं प्रवर्तयन्मनः कदा सदाशिवं भजे ॥**

> Drishad-Vichitra-Talpayoh Bhujanga-Mauktika-Masrajoh
>
> Garishta-Ratna-Loshthayoh Suhrid-Vipaksha-Pakshayoh
>
> Truna-Ravindra-Chakshoshoha Prajaa-Mahi-Mahendrayoho
>
> Samam Pravartayanmanaha Kada Sada-Shivam Bhaje

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Meaning

Between a bed of hard stone and a luxurious couch, adorned with garlands made of snakes and pearls, between precious gems and lumps of clay, and amidst friends and enemies alike (there resides Lord Shiva).

Between the sight of a blade of grass and a lotus, and between ordinary people and great kings, I worship the eternal Shiva, whose mind remains equanimous in all these circumstances.

- **र्भुजंगमौक्तिकमस्र (Bhujanga-Mauktika-Masrajoh): Garland made of pearls and snakes**

  - भुजंग (Bhujanga): Snake

  - मौक्तिक (Mauktika): Pearl

  - मस्रजः (Masrajah): Garland

- **जोर्गरिष्ठरत्नलोष्ठयोः (Garishta-Ratna-Loshthayoh): Between precious gems and lumps of clay**

  - गरिष्ठ (Garishta): Heavy, precious

  - रत्न (Ratna): Gem

  - लोष्ठ (Loshtha): Lump of clay

- **सुहृद्विपक्षपक्षयोः (Suhrid-Vipaksha-Pakshayoh): Between friends and enemies**

  - सुहृद् (Suhrid): Friend

  - विपक्ष (Vipaksha): Enemy

  - पक्ष (Paksha): Side

- **सदाशिवं (Sada-Shivam): The eternal Shiva**

- **भजे (Bhaje): Worship or meditate upon**

## Mythological Meaning

This verse illustrates Shiva’s transcendence above dualities, whether luxury or simplicity, wealth or poverty, friendship or enmity, or ordinary life versus royalty. Shiva embodies perfect calmness and impartiality.

Shiva’s garland of snakes interspersed with pearls symbolizes the coexistence of opposites in harmony. Snakes (danger) and pearls (beauty) together reflect his mastery over life’s paradoxes.

The verse also describes Lord Shiva as the one who remains unaffected by the external world. His detached yet compassionate nature highlights his role as the eternal observer and protector of all beings, transcending distinctions of rank, appearance, or status. The verse emphasizes Shiva’s yogic perfection, where he perceives everything equally, regardless of its intrinsic value or external appearance.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Spiritual Meaning

- **Practice Equanimity**: The verse teaches the importance of maintaining balance and impartiality. Whether we face comfort or hardship, honor, or insult, the path to peace lies in treating all circumstances with an equanimous mind. We have to keep in mind, that all situations and times are momentary, not permanent, and thereby avoid getting carried away with success and failure.

- **Harmony in Opposites**: Shiva’s garland of snakes and pearls reflects the unity in diversity. It reminds us to embrace all aspects of life, whether they appear threatening (like snakes) or alluring (like pearls).
