## Shiv Tandav Stotram

The [Shiv Tandav Stotram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva_Tandava_Stotra) is a powerful hymn dedicated to Lord Shiva, celebrating his cosmic dance, the Tandava. It is suspected to be composed by Ravana (not proven though), and it expresses awe for Shiva’s divine qualities, strength, and the profound energy of his dance, which symbolizes creation, preservation, and destruction. The storm serves as a prayer for spiritual awakening, invoking blessings of purity, power, and auspiciousness.

There are a total of [17 verses](https://hindi.webdunia.com/aarti-chalisa/shiv-tandav-stotram-114100100059_1.html) in the stotram. We will be exploring each verse separately and understanding its interpretation literally, mythologically as well as spiritually.

However, people might call it a destructive act but as with anything in the world, it depends on how you perceive it. If you take it positively it is a great source of inspiration and a heartful praise sung by the author (whoever it may be).

## Verse 6

> **ललाटचत्वरज्वल द्धनंजयस्फुलिंगभा**
>
> **निपीतपंच सायकंनम न्निलिंपनायकम्‌।**
>
> **सुधामयूखलेखया विराजमानशेखरं**
>
> **महाकपालिसंपदे शिरोजटालमस्तुनः ॥**
>
> Lalaata-Chatvarajwala Dhanjaya-Sphulingabhaa
>
> Nipita-Pancha Saayakam Nilimpa-Naayakam
>
> Sudha-Mayukha-Lekhaya Viraja-Mana-Shekharam
>
> Mahakapaali-Sampade Shiro-Jataala-Mastunaha

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Meaning

Whose forehead has the burning glow of the third eye, whose fire-like arrows destroy the god of love (Kaam Dev), salutations to the divine Lord Shiva.

Whose splendid crown has a beam of the life-giving stream (river Ganga), the great lord has hairs with matted locks that give prosperity to everyone.

## Mythological Meaning

The reference to arrows that destroy the god of love (Kaama dev) in the verse connects to the myth where Shiva burned Kama (the god of love) to ashes when disturbed by his desire for Parvati. This signifies Shiva's role as a protector against distractions and illusions.

The life-giving stream refers to the Ganga, which flows from Shiva’s matted locks. Shiva's association with the river symbolizes purity, life, and the removal of sins.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Spiritual Meaning

- **Transcend Attachments**: The arrows that destroy the god of love reflect the idea of overcoming worldly attachments and desires. In a spiritual context, this teaches the need to transcend physical desires to achieve higher states of awareness and inner peace.

<!-- -->

- **Dual Nature and natural** **balance**: The imagery of Shiva encapsulates the unity of opposites, destruction and creation, asceticism, and abundance. This duality is vital for understanding the nature of existence and finding balance in life.
