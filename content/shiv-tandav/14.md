## Shiv Tandav Stotram

The [Shiv Tandav Stotram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva_Tandava_Stotra) is a powerful hymn dedicated to Lord Shiva, celebrating his cosmic dance, the Tandava. It is suspected to be composed by Ravana (not proven though), and it expresses awe for Shiva’s divine qualities, strength, and the profound energy of his dance, which symbolizes creation, preservation, and destruction. The storm serves as a prayer for spiritual awakening, invoking blessings of purity, power, and auspiciousness.

There are a total of [17 verses](https://hindi.webdunia.com/aarti-chalisa/shiv-tandav-stotram-114100100059_1.html) in the stotram. We will be exploring each verse separately and understanding its interpretation literally, mythologically as well as spiritually.

However, people might call it a destructive act but as with anything in the world, it depends on how you perceive it. If you take it positively it is a great source of inspiration and a heartful praise sung by the author (whoever it may be).

## Verse 14

> **निलिम्प नाथनागरी कदम्ब मौलमल्लिका-**
>
> **निगुम्फनिर्भक्षरन्म धूष्णिकामनोहरः ।**
>
> **तनोतु नो मनोमुदं विनोदिनींमहनिशं**
>
> **परिश्रय परं पदं तदङ्गजत्विषां चयः ॥**

> Nilimapa Naatha-Naagari Kadamba Mauli-Mallika
>
> Nigumpha-Nirbharaksharanam Dhushnikaa-Manoharah
>
> Tanotu No Mano-Mudam Vindodinim-Mahar-Nisham
>
> Parishraya Param Padam Tadanga-Jatvisham Chayah

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Meaning

The celestial city of Shiva is adorned with garlands of kadamba and jasmine flowers on the divine crown, intertwined and emitting an enchanting fragrance like sweet nectar, captivating to the senses.

May it bring joy to our minds, a perpetual delight that lasts day and night, and it leads us to the supreme abode and the brilliance emanating from Parvati, the consort of Shiva.

## Word by Word Meaning

> **निलिम्प नाथनागरी कदम्ब मौलमल्लिका-**
>
> **निगुम्फनिर्भक्षरन्म धूष्णिकामनोहरः**

> Nilimapa Naatha-Naagari Kadamba Mauli-Mallika
>
> Nigumpha-Nirbharaksharanam Dhushnikaa-Manoharah

The celestial city of Shiva is adorned with garlands of kadamba and jasmine flowers on the divine crown, intertwined and emitting an enchanting fragrance like sweet nectar, captivating to the senses.

- **निलिम्पनाथनागरी (Nilimpa-Natha-Nagari): The celestial city of the Lord of gods (Shiva).**

  - निलिम्प (Nilimpa): Celestial beings or gods.

  - नाथ (Natha): Lord or master.

  - नागरी (Nagari): City.

- **कदम्बमौलमल्लिका (Kadamba-Maula-Mallika): Garland of kadamba flowers adorning the crown.**

  - कदम्ब (Kadamba): A type of fragrant flower.

  - मौल (Maula): Crown or head.

  - मल्लिका (Mallika): Jasmine flowers or a floral garland.

- **निगुम्फनिर्भक्षरन्म (Nigumpha-Nirbhaksharanam): Exuding sweet and enchanting**

  - निगुम्फ (Nigumpha): Clustered or intertwined.

  - निर्भक्षरन् (Nirbhaksharan): Emitting or radiating.

- **धूष्णिकामनोहरः (Madhushnika-Manoharah): Nectar-like fragrance.**

  - मधूष्णिका (Madhushnika): Nectar-like sweetness or fragrance.

  - मनोहरः (Manoharah): Delightful or captivating.

> **तनोतु नो मनोमुदं विनोदिनींमहनिशं**
>
> **परिश्रय परं पदं तदङ्गजत्विषां चयः**

> Tanotu No Mano-Mudam Vindodinim-Mahanisham
>
> Parishraya Param Padam Tadanga-Jatvisham Chayah

May it bring joy to our minds, a perpetual delight that lasts day and night, and it leads us to the supreme abode and the brilliance emanating from Parvati, the consort of Shiva.

- **तनोतु (Tanotu): May (it) bestow or spread.**

- **नो (No): To us.**

- **मनोमुदं (Mano-Mudam): Joy or bliss of the mind.**

- **विनोदिनींमहनिशं (Vinodinim-Ahanisham): Perpetual joy, day and night.**

  - विनोदिनीं (Vinodineem): Playful or joyous.

  - महनिशं (Ahanisham): Day and night.

- **परिश्रय (Parishraya): Shelter or refuge.**

- **परं पदं (Param Padam): The highest state or supreme abode.**

- **तदङ्गजत्विषां चयः (Tad-Angaja-Tvishaam Chayah): That cluster of brilliance emanating from Shiva's consort (Parvati).**

  - तद (Tad): That.

  - अङ्गज (Angaja): Refers to Parvati (daughter of Himavan, symbolizing her origin from divine form).

  - त्विषां (Tvisham): Brilliance or radiance.

  - चयः (Chayah): Cluster or collection.

## Mythological Meaning

The verse describes Shiva’s crown adorned with fragrant flowers, symbolizing simplicity, purity, and natural beauty despite his cosmic power. Also, it depicts the nectar-like fragrance representing the divine essence that pervades the universe, symbolizing Shiva’s omnipresence and the bliss derived from devotion to him.

The reference to constant joy signifies the eternal bliss that a devotee experiences when connected to the divine. Parvati's brilliance (as Angaja) highlights the balance of energy (Shakti) with Shiva, emphasizing their unity in sustaining cosmic harmony.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Spiritual Meaning

- **Divine Omnipresence**: The nectar-like essence symbolizes Shiva’s pervasive presence in every aspect of existence, teaching us to see divinity in all.

- **Union of Dualities**: The mention of Parvati’s radiance alongside Shiva reflects the harmony of masculine and feminine energies, urging us to balance aspects of strength and compassion in life.
