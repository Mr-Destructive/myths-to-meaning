## Shiv Tandav Stotram

The [Shiv Tandav Stotram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva_Tandava_Stotra) is a powerful hymn dedicated to Lord Shiva, celebrating his cosmic dance, the Tandava. It is suspected to be composed by Ravana (not proven though), and it expresses awe for Shiva’s divine qualities, strength, and the profound energy of his dance, which symbolizes creation, preservation, and destruction. The storm serves as a prayer for spiritual awakening, invoking blessings of purity, power, and auspiciousness.

There are a total of [17 verses](https://hindi.webdunia.com/aarti-chalisa/shiv-tandav-stotram-114100100059_1.html) in the stotram. We will be exploring each verse separately and understanding its interpretation literally, mythologically as well as spiritually.

However, people might call it a destructive act but as with anything in the world, it depends on how you perceive it. If you take it positively it is a great source of inspiration and a heartful praise sung by the author (whoever it may be).

## Verse 5

> **सहस्रलोचन प्रभृत्यशेषलेखशेखर**
>
> **प्रसूनधूलिधोरणी विधूसरां घ्रिपीठभूः।**
>
> **भुजंगराजमालया निबद्धजाटजूटकः**
>
> **श्रियैचिरायजायतां चकोरबंधुशेखरः ॥**
>
> Sahastra-Lochana Prabhutya-Lekha-Shekhara
>
> Prasuna-Dhuli-Dhorini Vidhu-Saraam Gri-Pi-Thabhuhu
>
> Bhujanga-Raja-Maalayaa Nibadha-Jaata-Jutakaha
>
> Shreyai-Chiraya-Jayataam Chakora-Bandhu-Shekhharaha

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Meaning

The Lord with a thousand eyes, whose crest is adorned with endless lines, whose feet have the sweet smell of the flowers and the flow of radiant dust at his feet.

Whose matted locks are bound by the garland of the king of serpents, he has the mythical bird chakora as his friend (moonlight). May the devotee be blessed with lasting prosperity.

## Mythological Meaning

Lord Shiva is often depicted as having a thousand eyes to symbolize his omnipresence and omniscience. The verse also praises Lord Shiva who controls the king of serpents emphasizing his supreme control over the forces of nature, including the primordial forces symbolized by the serpents.

The verse evokes the image of the earth being covered with the dust of divine flowers, symbolizing purity, prosperity, and divine blessings. The earth is sanctified by the grace of the goddess Lakshmi, who is often associated with wealth and fortune. The earth is seen as a radiant and fragrant place blessed by the divine, indicating the positive, life-giving energy flowing through it.

The Chakora bird is often associated with the moon in Hindu mythology. The moon represents Chandra, the lunar deity, or the cooling, soothing power of the divine. In the context of Lord Shiva, the imagery of the Chakora bird suggests their connection to the moon and the night, both symbols of tranquility, purity, and divine light. In this verse, Lord Shiva is described as the friend of the Chakora, meaning they are the source of all light and beauty, similar to how the moon illuminates the night sky

## Spiritual Meaning

- **Divine Vision and Omniscient Awareness**: The verse speaks of a being with a thousand eyes, symbolizing the all-seeing nature of the Divine. This reminds us that the Divine is ever-watchful, fully aware of our actions, thoughts, and struggles. Spiritually, it teaches us to embrace the Divine’s constant presence in our lives, knowing that nothing is beyond its awareness. As we cultivate this awareness, we begin to live with a greater sense of mindfulness, understanding that the Divine is guiding us through every moment.

- **Divine and Inner Purity**: The imagery of the earth, bathed in the dust of divine flowers, represents the purification and grace that the Divine bestows upon the world. This teaches us that when we purify our hearts and align ourselves with divine principles, we become channels for blessings.
