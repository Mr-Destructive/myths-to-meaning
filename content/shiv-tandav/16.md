## Shiv Tandav Stotram

The [Shiv Tandav Stotram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva_Tandava_Stotra) is a powerful hymn dedicated to Lord Shiva, celebrating his cosmic dance, the Tandava. It is suspected to be composed by Ravana (not proven though), and it expresses awe for Shiva’s divine qualities, strength, and the profound energy of his dance, which symbolizes creation, preservation, and destruction. The storm serves as a prayer for spiritual awakening, invoking blessings of purity, power, and auspiciousness.

There are a total of [17 verses](https://hindi.webdunia.com/aarti-chalisa/shiv-tandav-stotram-114100100059_1.html) in the stotram. We will be exploring each verse separately and understanding its interpretation literally, mythologically as well as spiritually.

However, people might call it a destructive act but as with anything in the world, it depends on how you perceive it. If you take it positively it is a great source of inspiration and a heartful praise sung by the author (whoever it may be).

## Verse 16

> **इमं हि नित्यमेवमुक्तमुत्तमोत्तमं स्तवं**
>
> **पठन्स्मरन्ब्रुवन्नरो विशुद्धिमेतिसंततम् ।**
>
> **हरे गुरौ सुभक्तिमाशु याति नान्यथा गतिं**
>
> **विमोहनं हि देहिनां सुशङ्करस्य चिंतनम् ॥**

> Imam Hi Nitya-Meva-Mukta-Mutta-Mottam Stavam
>
> Pathan-Smaran-Bruvanaro Vishudhi-Meti-Santatam
>
> Hare Gurao Shu-Bhakti-Mashu Yaati Nanyatha Gatim
>
> Vimohanam Hi Dehinam Shu-Shankarasya Chintanam

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Meaning

This most excellent hymn, as it has been spoken, if recited regularly and with devotion, a person, by reciting, remembering, and chanting it continuously, attains purity of mind and heart.

One quickly attains deep devotion to Lord Shiva (the ultimate Guru) and finds no other path or purpose, meditating on the auspicious Lord Shiva removes the delusions of all embodied beings.

## Word by Word Meaning

> इमं हि नित्यमेवमुक्तमुत्तमोत्तमं स्तवं
>
> पठन्स्मरन्ब्रुवन्नरो विशुद्धिमेतिसंततम्

> Imam Hi Nitya-Meva-Mukta-Mutta-Mottam Stavam
>
> Pathan-Smaran-Bruvanaro Vishudhi-Meti-Santatam

This most excellent hymn, as it has been spoken, if recited regularly and with devotion, a person, by reciting, remembering, and chanting it continuously, attains purity of mind and heart.

- **इमं (Imam): This (refers to the hymn or stotra)**

- **हि (Hi): Indeed, certainly**

- **नित्यमेवमुक्तमुत्तमोत्तमं (Nityam-Evam-Uktam-Uttamottamam):** It serves the best when recited as it is and regularly

  - नित्यम् (Nityam): Daily, regularly, always

  - एवम् (Evam): In this manner, as said

  - उक्तम् (Uktam): Spoken, declared

  - उत्तमोत्तमं (Uttamottamam): The best of the best, supremely excellent

- **स्तवं (Stavam): Hymn or praise**

- **पठन्स्मरन्ब्रुवन्नरो (Pathan-Smaran-Bruvan-Naro): A person who recites**, **remembers** **(mentally meditates), and chants**

  - पठन् (Pathan): Reciting

  - स्मरन् (Smaran): Remembering, meditating upon

  - ब्रुवन् (Bruvan): Speaking or chanting aloud

  - नरः (Narah): A person, human being

- **विशुद्धिमेतिसंततम् (Vishuddhim-Eti-Santatam): Attains purity continuously**

  - विशुद्धिम् (Vishuddhim): Purity, purification

  - एति (Eti): Attains

  - संततम् (Santatam): Continuously, always.

> हरे गुरौ सुभक्तिमाशु याति नान्यथा गतिं
>
> विमोहनं हि देहिनां सुशङ्करस्य चिंतनम्

> Hare Gurao Shu-Bhakti-Mashu Yaati Nanyatha Gatim
>
> Vimohanam Hi Dehinam Shu-Shankarasya Chintanam

One quickly attains deep devotion to Lord Shiva (the ultimate Guru) and finds no other path or purpose, meditating on the auspicious Lord Shiva removes the delusions of all embodied beings.

- **हरे (Hare): Refers to the Lord (Shiva or Vishnu, depending on the context)**

- **गुरौ (Gurau): To the Guru (teacher or spiritual guide).**

- **सुभक्तिमाशु (Su-Bhaktim-Aashu): Deep and quick devotion**

  - सुभक्तिम् (Su-Bhaktim): Deep devotion

  - आशु (Aashu): Quickly, promptly

- **याति (Yati): Attains, reaches**

- **नान्यथा (Naanyathaa): Not in another way**

  - न (Na): Not

  - अन्यथा (Anyathaa): Otherwise, in another way

- **गतिं (Gatim): Path, state, or destination**

- **विमोहनं (Vimohanam): Removal of delusion or confusion**

- **हि (Hi): Indeed, certainly**

- **देहिनां (Dehinaam): Of embodied beings (those with physical bodies)**

- **सुशङ्करस्य (Sushankarasya): Of the auspicious Shiva**

- **चिंतनम् (Chintanam): Contemplation or meditation.**

## Mythological Meaning

The verse highlights Shiva as "Hara" (remover of sins and ignorance) and as the divine Guru, emphasizing his dual role as both destroyer of delusion and the guide to liberation.

The act of meditating on Shiva symbolizes connecting to the eternal consciousness, which dissolves worldly attachments and illusions (Maya). Also by chanting and reflecting on Shiva’s virtues, a devotee achieves inner purity and deep devotion, essential steps toward self-realization.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Spiritual Meaning

- **Consistency in Spiritual Practice**: Regular recitation and meditation on divine virtues lead to spiritual progress and mental clarity.

- **Guidance of the Guru**: Surrendering to a higher spiritual guide (Shiva as the Guru) is crucial for finding one's true path and purpose in life.

- **Breaking Illusions**: Meditation on Shiva helps transcend the illusions of the material world, leading to liberation from suffering and ignorance.
