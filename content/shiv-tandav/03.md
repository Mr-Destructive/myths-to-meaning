## Shiv Tandav Stotram

The [Shiv Tandav Stotram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva_Tandava_Stotra) is a powerful hymn dedicated to Lord Shiva, celebrating his cosmic dance, the Tandava. It is suspected to be composed by Ravana (not proven though), and it expresses awe for Shiva’s divine qualities, strength, and the profound energy of his dance, which symbolizes creation, preservation, and destruction. The storm serves as a prayer for spiritual awakening, invoking blessings of purity, power, and auspiciousness.

There are a total of [17 verses](https://hindi.webdunia.com/aarti-chalisa/shiv-tandav-stotram-114100100059_1.html) in the stotram. We will be exploring each verse separately and understanding its interpretation literally, mythologically as well as spiritually.

However, people might call it a destructive act but as with anything in the world, it depends on how you perceive it. If you take it positively it is a great source of inspiration and a heartful praise sung by the author (whoever it may be).

## Verse 3

> **धराधरेंद्रनंदिनी विलासबन्धुबन्धुर  
> स्फुरद्दिगंतसंतति प्रमोद मानमानसे।**
>
>   
> **कृपाकटाक्षधोरणी निरुद्धदुर्धरापदि  
> क्वचिद्विगम्बरे मनोविनोदमेतु वस्तुनि ।।**
>
> Dhara-Dharendra-Nandini Vilaas-Bandhu-Bandhura
>
> Sphuradhi-Ganta-Santati Promod Maana-Maanse
>
> Krupa-Kataksha-Dhorini Niruddha-Durddhara-Apadi
>
> Kvachid-Digambare Mano-Vinoda-Metu Vastuni

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Meaning

The beautiful Sporting consort of the daughter of the mountain king, whose glory radiates in all directions, brings joy to my heart and mind.

Whose glances are like a stream of mercy removing the obstacles or calamities, let my mind find delight in Lord Shiva who is present everywhere.

## Word by Word Meaning

> **धराधरेंद्रनंदिनी विलासबन्धुबन्धुर**
>
> **स्फुरद्दिगंतसंतति प्रमोद मानमानसे।**
>
> Dhara-Dharendra-Nandini Vilaas-Bandhu-Bandhura
>
> Sphuradhi-Ganta-Santati Promod Maana-Maanse

The beautiful Sporting consort of the daughter of the mountain king, whose glory radiates in all directions, brings joy to my heart and mind

- **धराधरेंद्रनंदिनी (Dhara-Dharendra-Nandini): The daughter of king of mountains who brings joy (here it refers to Goddess Parvati, the consort of Lord Shiva)**

  - धराधर (Dharadhara): Mountains

  - इन्द्र (Indra): King

  - नंदिनी (Nandini): daughter or one who brings joy.

- **विलासबन्धुबन्धुर (Vilaas-Bandhu-Bandhura):**

  - विलास (Vilāsa): Grace, beauty, or play

  - बन्धु (Bandhu): Relative or Friend

  - बन्धुर (Bandhura): One who is lovable, endearing, or affectionate

- **स्फुरद्दिगंतसंतति (Sphuradhi-Ganta-Santati):**

  - स्फुरत् (Sphurat): Shining, radiating

  - दिगंत (Diganta): Horizon, directions (literally means the end or boundary of the directions, i.e., the sky)

  - संतति (Santati): Lineage, continuity, extending

- **प्रमोद (Promoda): Joy, delight**

- **मानमानसे (Maana-Maanase):**

  - मान (Maana): Respect, pride

  - मानसे (Maanase): In the heart or mind

> **कृपाकटाक्षधोरणी निरुद्धदुर्धरापदि**
>
> **क्वचिद्विगम्बरे मनोविनोदमेतु वस्तुनि**
>
> Krupa-Kataksha-Dhorini Niruddha-Durddhara-Apadi
>
> Kvachidhi-Gambare Mano-Vinoda-Metu Vastuni

Whose glances are like a stream of mercy removing the obstacles or calamities, let my mind find delight in Lord Shiva

- **कृपाकटाक्षधोरणी (**Krupa-Kataksha-Dhorini)

  - कृपा (Krupa): Mercy, compassion, grace

  - कटाक्ष (Kataksha): Side-glance (often used to denote a compassionate or affectionate glance)

  - धोरणी (Dhorani): Stream, flow, the one who holds or supports

- **निरुद्धदुर्धरापदि (**Niruddha-Durddhara-Apadi):

  - निरुद्ध (Niruddha): Prevented, stopped

  - दुर्धरा (Durddhara): Difficult to overcome, hard to tackle

  - आपदि (Apadi): In danger or calamity

- **क्वचिद्विगम्बरे (**Kvachidhi-Gambare):

  - क्वचित (Kvachita): Sometimes, occasionally

  - दिगम्बरे (Digambare): The sky, or the cosmic space (here, one who wears the sky, Lord Shiva, indicating his presence is everywhere)

- **मनोविनोदमेतु (**Mano-Vinoda-Metu):

  - **मनो** (Mano): Mind, thought

  - **विनोद** (Vinoda): Pleasure, delight

  - **एतु** (Etu): May it come

- **वस्तुनि (Vastuni):** Things, objects, or situations

## Mythological Meaning

Lord Shiva is often depicted as the ultimate protector who removes obstacles (Vighnaharta). His merciful glance and compassion can dissolve the most difficult problems his devotees face. As the one present everywhere, Shiva transcends the material world and is the source of infinite wisdom and peace. His glance is said to purify the mind and spirit, leading to liberation.

Goddess Parvati, as the daughter of the Himalayas, embodies divine feminine energy, love, grace, and nurturing. She is often considered the source of all power (Shakti) and the consort of Lord Shiva, the embodiment of consciousness and asceticism. Together, they symbolize the balance of creation, preservation, and destruction in the universe.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Spiritual Meaning

- **Mercy and Grace**: The verse emphasizes that Lord Shiva’s compassionate glance has the power to remove the most difficult obstacles in life. In spiritual terms, this represents the transformative power of grace. When a devotee is troubled by worldly challenges or inner turmoil, invoking Shiva’s grace through meditation, prayer, or contemplation helps to dissolve these obstacles.

- **The Infinite Radiance**: Goddess Parvati’s radiance is described as spreading across all directions, symbolizing the omnipresent nature of Shakti, the divine energy. By recognizing and aligning with this inner energy (the Divine Feminine), a practitioner can experience true spiritual bliss and mental clarity.
