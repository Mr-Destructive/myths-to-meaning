## Shiv Tandav Stotram

The [Shiv Tandav Stotram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva_Tandava_Stotra) is a powerful hymn dedicated to Lord Shiva, celebrating his cosmic dance, the Tandava. It is suspected to be composed by Ravana (not proven though), and it expresses awe for Shiva’s divine qualities, strength, and the profound energy of his dance, which symbolizes creation, preservation, and destruction. The storm serves as a prayer for spiritual awakening, invoking blessings of purity, power, and auspiciousness.

There are a total of [17 verses](https://hindi.webdunia.com/aarti-chalisa/shiv-tandav-stotram-114100100059_1.html) in the stotram. We will be exploring each verse separately and understanding its interpretation literally, mythologically as well as spiritually.

However, people might call it a destructive act but as with anything in the world, it depends on how you perceive it. If you take it positively it is a great source of inspiration and a heartful praise sung by the author (whoever it may be).

## Verse 7

> **करालभालपट्टिका धगद्धगद्धगज्ज्वल**
>
> **द्धनंजया धरीकृतप्रचंड पंचसायके।**
>
> **धराधरेंद्रनंदिनी कुचाग्रचित्रपत्र**
>
> **कप्रकल्पनैकशिल्पिनी त्रिलोचनेरतिर्मम ॥**
>
> Karaal-Bhaala-Pattika Dhagad-Dhagad-Dhagajwala
>
> Dhananjayaa Dhari-Kruta-Prachanda Pancha-Saayake
>
> Dhara-Dharendra-Nandini Kuchaagra-Chitra-Patra
>
> Kapra-Kalpa-Naika-Shilpini Tri-Lochane-Ratir-Mama

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Meaning

The one whose forehead has a fierce band of fire that creates an intense sound along with the brightness of the burning glow, the one who has conquered the wealth and who offered the fierce five arrows to the Lord of Love (Kaama Deva)

Whose consort is the daughter of the king of mountains, who decorates her dress with beautiful pictures and leaves, who is the artist of that unique manifestation or design, I bow down to the three-eyed Lord (Lord Shiva) with devotion.

## Mythological Meaning

The imagery of a fierce forehead decoration evokes Lord Shiva’s fierce aspect, often represented in his form as Bhairava or other fierce deities. This symbolizes his power to protect the universe and destroy evil forces. In many myths, Shiva’s third eye signifies his ability to see beyond the physical realm and perceive deeper truths.

The verse also references Parvati, who is the daughter of the mountain (Himalayas). In mythology, Parvati represents Shakti, the divine feminine energy, and is often depicted as the nurturing and compassionate aspect of the divine. Her connection to the mountains signifies stability, strength, and the eternal nature of the universe. Her relationship with Shiva embodies the union of opposites masculine and feminine energies.

This verse also highlights the creative power of the divine. Creation is often associated with divine artisanship, to shape the universe through their will.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Spiritual Meaning

- **Embracing Fierceness and Strength**: The imagery of a fierce forehead and blazing presence symbolizes the power of confronting challenges in life. This teaches us to harness our inner strength and face our fears with courage.

- **Artistry of Creation and Individual Potential**: Lord Shiva as the unique artist reflects the idea that each individual has the potential to create and shape their own destiny. Spiritually, this encourages us to recognize our creative power and the role we play in the universe. It reminds us that our thoughts, actions, and intentions shape our reality.

- **Devotion and Surrender:** It also teaches us that true spiritual progress comes from humility and the recognition of a higher power. By cultivating devotion, we open ourselves to divine guidance and grace, allowing for transformation and growth.
