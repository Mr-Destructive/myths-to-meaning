## Shiv Tandav Stotram

The [Shiv Tandav Stotram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva_Tandava_Stotra) is a powerful hymn dedicated to Lord Shiva, celebrating his cosmic dance, the Tandava. It is suspected to be composed by Ravana (not proven though), and it expresses awe for Shiva’s divine qualities, strength, and the profound energy of his dance, which symbolizes creation, preservation, and destruction. The storm serves as a prayer for spiritual awakening, invoking blessings of purity, power, and auspiciousness.

There are a total of [17 verses](https://hindi.webdunia.com/aarti-chalisa/shiv-tandav-stotram-114100100059_1.html) in the stotram. We will be exploring each verse separately and understanding its interpretation literally, mythologically as well as spiritually.

However, people might call it a destructive act but as with anything in the world, it depends on how you perceive it. If you take it positively it is a great source of inspiration and a heartful praise sung by the author (whoever it may be).

## Verse 17

> **पूजावसानसमये दशवक्त्रगीतं**
>
> **यः शम्भुपूजनपरं पठति प्रदोषे ।**
>
> **तस्य स्थिरां रथगजेन्द्रतुरङ्गयुक्तां**
>
> **लक्ष्मीं सदैव सुमुखिं प्रददाति शम्भुः ॥**

> Pujaa-Vasaan-Samaye Dasha-Vaktra-Geetam
>
> Yah Shambhu-Pujanam-Param Pathati Pradoshe
>
> Tasya Stiraam Ratha-Gajendra-Turnga-Yuktam
>
> Lakshimim Sadaev Sumukhim Pradadati Shambhuh

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Meaning

Whoever recites this hymn by Ravana at the end of Shiva’s worship during the Pradosha time, Lord Shiva always grants them unwavering wealth, symbolized by chariots, elephants, horses, and the favor of Goddess Lakshmi.

## Word by Word Meaning

> **पूजावसानसमये दशवक्त्रगीतं**
>
> **यः शम्भुपूजनपरं पठति प्रदोषे**

> Pujaa-Vasaan-Samaye Dasha-Vaktra-Geetam
>
> Yah Shambhu-Pujanam-Param Pathati Pradoshe

Whoever recites this hymn by Ravana at the end of Shiva’s worship during the Pradosha time…

- **पूजावसानसमये (Pujaa-Avasaana): At the time when worship ends**

  - पूजा (Puja): Worship or prayer

  - अवसान (Avasaana): End or conclusion

  - समये (Samaye): Time or moment.

- **दशवक्त्रगीतं (Dasha-Vaktra-Gitam): The hymn composed by the ten-headed Ravana**

  - दश (Dasha): Ten

  - वक्त्र (Vaktra): Face or head

  - गीतं (Gītam): Song or hymn

- **यः (Yah): Whoever**

- **शम्भुपूजनपरं (Shambhu-Pujana-Param): Devoted to worshiping Lord Shiva**

  - शम्भु (Shambhu): Lord Shiva

  - पूजन (Pujana): Worship or offering

  - परं (Param): Devoted to or focused on

- **पठति (Pathati): Recites or chants.**

- **प्रदोषे (Pradoshe): Twilight time, especially after sunset, is considered sacred for Shiva worship**

> **तस्य स्थिरां रथगजेन्द्रतुरङ्गयुक्तां**
>
> **लक्ष्मीं सदैव सुमुखिं प्रददाति शम्भुः**

> Tasya Sthiraam Ratha-Gajendra-Turnga-Yuktaam
>
> Lakshimim Sadaeva Sumukhim Pradadati Shambhuh

Lord Shiva always grants them unwavering wealth, symbolized by chariots, elephants, horses, and the favor of Goddess Lakshmi

- **तस्य (Tasya): To that person**

- **स्थिरां (Sthiraam): Steady, fixed, or permanent**

- **रथगजेन्द्रतुरङ्गयुक्तां (Ratha-Gajendra-Turanga-Yuktaam): Equipped with chariots, elephants, and horses (symbols of wealth and status)**

  - रथ (Ratha): Chariot

  - गज (Gaja): Elephant

  - इन्द्र (Indra): Chief or lord (indicating grandeur)

  - तुरङ्ग (Turanga): Horse

  - युक्तां (Yuktaam): Connected with or equipped with

- **लक्ष्मीं (Lakshimim): Goddess Lakshmi, the deity of wealth and prosperity**

- **सदैव (Sadaeva): Always or perpetually**

  - सदा (Sada): Always

    एव (Eva): Indeed, emphasizing continuity

- **सुमुखिं (Sumukhim): With a kind and pleasant face, indicating favor and blessings**

  - सु (Su): Good, beautiful, or pleasant

    मुख (Mukha): Face

- **प्रददाति (Pradadati): Grants or bestows**

  - प्र (Pra): Fully or completely

  - ददाति (Dadati): Gives or grants

- **शम्भुः (Shambhuh): Lord Shiva, the bestower of blessings**

## Mythological Meaning

Ravana, despite being a demon king and often depicted as an antagonist, was a great devotee of Lord Shiva. This hymn represents his unwavering devotion, showcasing that true worship transcends moral or ethical labels. The mention of Ravana emphasizes the power of sincere devotion, regardless of one’s background or past actions.

The Pradosha time is considered the most auspicious time to worship Lord Shiva. Mythology states that during this time, the gods and sages worship Shiva to seek his blessings, making it an ideal moment to recite hymns like this one.

Lord Shiva is known as "Ashutosh," meaning one who is easily pleased. This verse illustrates his readiness to bestow material and spiritual blessings on his devotees when approached with devotion and sincerity.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Spiritual Meaning

- **Importance of Devotion Over Status**: Ravana’s devotion shows that divine grace is accessible to anyone, regardless of their perceived morality or societal standing, as long as their worship is sincere.

- **Aligning with Auspicious Moments**: Pradosha represents a time when cosmic energies align, emphasizing the significance of timing in spiritual practices for enhanced results.

- **Spiritual Wealth Over Material Wealth**: While the verse highlights material blessings like wealth and prosperity, it symbolizes the higher spiritual wealth of Shiva’s grace, which leads to contentment, stability, and liberation.

- **Consistency in Worship**: The regular recitation of hymns, particularly during auspicious times, fosters spiritual discipline and helps devotees remain connected to the divine.

- **Shiva as the Ultimate Bestower**: Shiva's blessings are not limited to material prosperity but extend to spiritual growth, reminding devotees to seek his grace for inner peace and liberation.
