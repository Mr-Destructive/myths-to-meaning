We are entering the second phase of Navratri, of the three phases. It is the fourth day of Navratri.

In the first three days, we tried to balance courage and peace. We tried to overcome the negative emotions in us that kept us from doing our actions. Moving on, we need to understand that there are forces in nature that are sometimes not in our control, and however we might try to stop it, it would cease to resist, it will happen to us, the nature of emotions is such. You can’t say something is completely wrong or something is completely right, nothing is perfect, everything has duality. The more you try to break the myth, the more it breaks into two emotions, happiness and sadness.

The fourth day, as the beginning of the second phase however is about the motherly bond that mother nature has towards all the living being. This is the exact form of Maa Parvati, Maa Kushmanda that fosters motherly aspects, depicts the selfless love, kindness, determination to protect and raise her children and the most important one harnessing creativity in doing so.

## Maa Kushmanda

Goddess Kushmanda embodies its power with the center of the sun, serving as the source of all life and energy. She possesses the ability to restore cosmic balance through the sun’s rays, representing energy (Shakti).

Meanwhile, two asuras, Mali and Sumali, who were the sons of the warrior Sukesh, performed an intense penance to win Lord Shiva’s attention. Their meditation emitted such a glow that Suryadev (Sun), curious, descended to Earth to investigate.

Ignoring celestial laws, Suryadev approached the glowing brothers, inadvertently causing their destruction by turning them to ashes. Furious, Shiva threw his Trishul at Suryadev, plunging the universe into darkness. This broke the balance of gravity, causing chaos, prompting Rishi Kashyapa to curse Shiva, declaring he would also lose a son. Seeking redemption, Shiva urged Parvati to help restore Suryadev.

Sukesh and Rishi Kashyapa arrived, with Sukesh in tears and Rishi Kashyapa furious. After consulting Parvati, who instructed them to fill one pot with their blood, they magically filled another with Amrit. She asked Rishi Kashyapa to pour both into Suryadev, promising to bear a great child in return. Parvati then gifted Devavati an egg, which hatched into demonic offspring. Despite this, she earned the name Kushmanda for her virtuous actions, and Suryadev welcomed her into Suryalok, where her brilliance was essential for guiding the Sun and illuminating the universe.

## Embracing Creativity

Maa Kushmanda’s story begins with an act of kindness, not a war cry, quite the opposite in fact, a smile that gave birth to the universe. From complete darkness, she radiated energy so potent it set the entire cosmos in motion. Creation didn’t come from force, it came from a subtle spark of joy and intention.

This reminds us that creativity does not need to be loud or perfect. Often, it’s born from silence, a small inner impulse or intuition. In our own lives, we wait for the “right moment” to start, to express, to create. Maa Kushmanda teaches that we are never empty. We only need to believe in that first smile, the intuition, the first guess, that initial idea, act of courage, or word of kindness, that can set everything else into motion.

## Nurturing Kindness

When Shiva struck Suryadev, it was Maa Kushmanda who stepped forward, not with blame, but with kindness and forgiveness. She didn’t just restore balance, she did it by healing others, by offering herself in service, being selfless, just as a mother has always been.

In a world where anger is often louder than empathy, her story reminds us that kindness is not weakness. To nurture is not just to care, it is to be strong enough to support others even when you are holding your own burdens. Her act of restoring Suryadev through blood and Amrit symbolizes the balance between sacrifice and grace, justice and forgiveness.

Today, when you face the dilemma of intuition to act kind over the ego that kindles softness as weakness, or expressing kindness over rage, Maa Kushmanda shows us that restoration happens through warmth, not through more destruction or egoistic thinking.

## Accepting the happenings

Despite her immense power, Maa Kushmanda does not try to control every event. She allows destruction to take its course and then steps in with awareness. Her wisdom lies in responding, not resisting, in performing her duties and not getting lost in everything.

This is perhaps the most challenging lesson for the modern mind, acceptance. Its not about letting things happen without taking action, but as an understanding that not everything is in our control, and that trying to force outcomes only deepens our suffering, what we control is just the act, and not its fruit. When events shake us, or cause dilemma which could be due to loss, heartbreak, or failure, we need to remember that the same force which creates the storm also holds the power to heal it.

By surrendering to the moment and staying centered like Maa Kushmanda, we find the strength to endure, respond, and create again.

## Meaning from the Myths

The myth of Maa Kushmanda is not just a tale of gods and demons, it is a symbolic journey of inner transformation. Her smile, the act of kindness represents that initial awakening, the moment you realize that even in darkness, you carry light. The asuras represent ego and ambition, whose unchecked force leads to imbalance or misery and lust for the desires. Shiva’s rage and Parvati’s grace show the tension between destruction and healing, and the egg given to Devavati symbolizes potential, the unpredictable yet fertile ground from which life can emerge.

So, the message is quite profound from the fourth day:

- Embrace your creativity wherever possible, don’t hold yourself back just because of fear or any emotion

- Be Kind and express gratitude in whatever the situation. Balance is key, but if life gives you a choice of anger or kindness, choose the later. Choose kindness. Anger always brings destruction within first then outwards.

- Give in to the situation and any challenge thrown at you. Don’t stop just because you are not responsible. Take ownership and act anyway. Don’t let the situation dictate the actions of your, adopt yes, but not to your soul’s core.

Hope, Maa Parvati brings joy and kindness in your life.
