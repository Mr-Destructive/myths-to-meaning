Day three it is, if you reached here, let me say that you have completed one third of the challenge, it’s just closer to the dawn of the morning you’ll be waiting for.

You've taken action. You've learned to focus and detach from the ego. But now comes perhaps the most challenging part of any transformative journey.

Maintaining your inner peace while facing the storms of life.

The world around you might become chaotic, people might not understand your path, and circumstances might test your resolve. This is where the third form of Maa Parvati, Maa Chandraghanta, helps us.

## Maa Chandraghanta

Maa Chandraghanta, what a beautiful name, just think for a moment about its meaning.

> Chandra → meaning moon
>
> Ghanta → meaning bell or sound of bell

This name might not make sense when given the actual meaning, but deeply think about the words and the meaning both elements in the name. One is peaceful, the other not as peaceful but soothing and alarming.

Moon is the peace element, the one that gives us calmness, and the bell is the one that alerts us, gives a sound of something happening, which is not calm.

Did you get that meaning? It’s quite beautifully showing the dual nature of Maa Parvati. One who can be calm as the moon, but can also transform into a more aggressive or non-peaceful form.

## Dual Nature of Maa Chandraghanta

The story of Maa Chandraghanta have a couple of different approaches, as there are nine forms and any of them can be related to anyone, since its Maa Parvati with her divine form.

### Transformation before Lord Shiva

One mythology is when Maa Parvati was successful in her penance and Lord Shiva was impressed with her focus and state of spiritual energy. They both were about to be married. So, lord Shiva, riding on his bull (Nandi), along with his ghosts and ghouls accompanying him, was approaching the entrance of the palace of Himavat. The palace where Maa Parvati resided. On seeing the ghostly tribes, with ash-covered bodies, snakes as ornaments, and ghosts and goblins, the humans and everyone around them were terrified.

On seeing the chaos, Maa Parvati transformed her energy and rage into a divine dual form, Devi/Maa Chandraghanta. She adorned herself with a half-moon on her forehead, which glowed with divine light and produced a melodious sound that calmed everyone's fears. Her golden complexion radiated peace, while her multiple arms held weapons, showing her transformation into her dual nature.

With her peaceful aura, she brought her dear humans back to normal, as she calmed everyone's fear with the beautiful, divine melody of her bell. On the other hand, the same melody of her bell was able to drive away demons and negativity, making the environment pure.

This transformation was not about killing or impressing someone; it was about embracing the fear and using it as a fuel to radiate energy and act righteously.

### Encounter with Jatukasura

There was a powerful asura named Jatukasura, among the many asuras. With his penance, he gained great powers. In his pride, he raised an army of giant bats. These bats caused destruction in villages, crops, and sacred rituals. The asura was causing a disturbance to sages and even gods, so they found the need to call Maa Parvati, Mother nature, for help.

She rode on her wolf/lion (not sure which, but mostly wolf). She is also called Vrikahvahini / Vrikahrudha, which means the one who rides on a wolf. She carried her weapons in various arms, carrying the bell in her forehead. The divine sound of her bell was enough to fend off and kill the humongous army of bats, and with her weapons and wolf, she was able to pierce the demon’s chest. Jatukasura fell dead.

This again goes to show the dual nature of Maa Chandraganta. She brings peace with her calm, soothing, mother-like nature and fierce as a bell’s voice to fend off evil and negative emotions.

## Developing Courage

Maa Chandraghanta teaches us a profound lesson about courage. True courage isn't about being fearless. It's about feeling fear and acting anyway.

If you have taken the first step, the second, and are now realising how hard your goal is?

Good, Congratulations, you are stepping out of your comfort zone, embrace the discomfort, let it settle, but don’t stop becuase of your fear of failure.

In the worst case, you will fail, true, but there will be something that comfort can’t replace, the transformation, the realisation of your potential, the knoweldge of once unknown, the expreince of completion, and if none of it resonates then the shear joy of doing it.

I often used to hold myself back when writing posts like this or even technical posts, but I have taken the courage and overcome the fear of being judged. I still continued writing and learning. No one is perfect, everyone is figuring things out, and in the worst case, you will learn something.

Courage can’t be only rage, it can be a form of discipline and self-care for developing the art or extending your skill out of the comfort zone.

## Inner Peace

Having courage does not only mean being ruthless or evil. It also means to balance it with inner peace, the core of one’s true nature.

Courage against evil is good, but having an unjustified rage on everyone is not ideal and can bring you in the opposite end of a peaceful position.

That is Maa Chandraghata’s example is so valuable; she righteously uses and directs her rage to ward off evil and bring peace to the people around her. Likewise, we can balance the emotion of rage and peace. Peace to the people around us who support us, who encourage us to do good things, who work for us, maybe they could be family, friends, teachers, relatives, etc. However, the people who bring us down, abuse children, use violence, disrespect women, and our dear ones, in those situations, expressing rage is valid and righteous.

While trying to do a lot of things, I feel a deep satisifaction, the inner peace is the thing that gets me writing the next word, flipping the next page, designing the architecture of the next software project, imagining the next scene and fictional scenarios, scriblling, all of them, among the others, they give me peace, a satisifaction from the core.

Find your satisifaction, just do it, there doesn’t have to be a world purpose in it, art doesn’t have a purpose until it is created.

## Meaning from the Myth

Maa Chandraghanta truly depicts the dual nature of energy. She encourages us to channel our energy for the betterment of ourselves and others. The crucial thing to take from here is balance, or the direction of the energy.

It shows us having a fearless approach but having a compassionate mind in our process. Both are necessary, otherwise, you might lose your wisdom in rage, or evil thoughts will consume your soft soul.

So, the third step is finding the balance from the past two days, the courage to take the first step, the next to focus and bring the inner peace, however, keeping up with both of them is vital, don’t get carried away with the emotion.
