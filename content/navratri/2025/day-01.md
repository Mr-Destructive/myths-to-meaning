## Maa Shailputri

Maa Shailputri, the daughter of King Himavat, yes, she is Parvati in its truest form. Shailputri is just a name that resembles her perfectly. How?

> Shaila → meaning Mountain
>
> Putri → meaning daughter
>
> Shailaputri → meaning the daughter of the mountain

King Himavat and Queen Meenavati are the lords of the mountain Himalayan region. After their penance, a boon was given (not clear, it’s a myth maybe) to them that their daughter will be born as a form of the Goddess.

And rightly so, after a few years, they conceived a daughter and named her Parvati (The daughter of Parvat Raj, Himavat, the king of mountains), among the other names:

- Parvati (The daughter of Parvat Raj, Himavat, the king of mountains)

- Himavati (The daughter of Himavat)

- Shailaja (The daughter of Mountains)

- Shailputri (The daughter of Mountains)

And one of the names I want to highlight here is her word “Uma”

> Uma → meaning “oh, don’t”

This fits this context well. Let me get back on this after a while. Let’s continue the story for now.

### Parvati’s Penance

One day, Sage Narada visited Parvati's parents, Himavat and Menavati, and foretold that their daughter was the reincarnation of Sati and destined to marry Shiva. From a young age, Parvati exhibited extraordinary devotion to Lord Shiva, often engaging in prayers and rituals dedicated to him. Upon hearing this, Parvati insisted that Sage Narada to tell more about how she can attain Lord Shiva, or rather convince him that she is indeed their Sati. Sage Narada instructed that the only way to convince and marry Shiva would be through extreme penance.

Determined, Parvati embarked on a rigorous path of penance. She chose a secluded spot in the Himalayas, known as Gauri Kund, to perform her austerities. Her penance was intense: she meditated amidst scorching flames during summer, sat on bare rocks during the rainy season, and immersed herself in freezing waters during winter. This unwavering dedication led to her being recognized as a symbol of devotion and perseverance.

> This is where her name Uma (oh don’t) name comes from. Her mother, Meenavati, upon hearing the vow of her daughter of penance, she was afraid and reluctant to let her go. She insisted her in not going. And that is how her name coms from “oh, don’t” go to perform your duties, just be who you are.

Can you relate?

This Meenavati, the mother of Parvati/Himavati/Shailputri, is like your mind, caring in excess, letting your potential in your head; this is your mind procrastinating and delaying the inevitable. But you have to be hear her and let her go, you are desitined to be great, you can’t momentarily pleasure block you from destined actions and duties. Your mind will cloud you with delusion, with comfort over action; don’t listen like Uma. Do the hard thing, ignore it, do what you are supposed to be doing. If confused, if lost, if not sure what to do, do what your heart says, even if mind interwines in your thoughts, let it ramble a bit, it will, at first, once, twice, again and again, but just listen and move on, I can distil a hope in you that there will be a day where the mind will do waht your heart wanted. It just is tied to the default version right now; let it burn in your penance.

### Embrace Discomfort

The first step is often discomfort, that’s what one of the names of Shailputri Maa, Uma, is. Despite everyone’s reluctance to let her do her actions, she bravely set out on the path of penance. And that’s how we know her today as one of the many forms of Jagdamba, Maa Parvati.

Discomfort is not bad, its just the start, we can’t judge something that has barely started. When we start we often have a pre-conceived notion of how things should be. The discomfort arises when we view our skills as not matching the pre-conceived notion of the existing people out there; it creates a gap of skill, often leading to dissatisfaction just on the thought of our skills, which prevents us from the act of doing it.

That’s why embracing that discomfort is important, Parvati/Shailputri realised it that her qualities like physical beauty are quite unmatched, but what the acquaintance of love, her Shiva, required was not materialistic beauty, but rather the inner purification and harmony of heart and mind.

Initially, Maa Shailputri was not good at doing penance; there were thoughts and negative emotions tied in her mind, but she embraced the fact that she was indeed not perfect, and kept on doing her penance regardless. Over time, she deepened her focus, cleansed her mind, detached from materialistic desires, and purified her emotions. She slowly started to become selfless, and finally, after years and years of practice and deep penance, she attained Lord Shiva (its a long story still, but out of this context).

## Meanings from the Myth

At the core, we can distil the meaning of the mythology of Maa Shailputri as

- The daughter of Mountains, depicting the vastness and strength

- Riding a bull, indicating the control of her senses

- Holding Lotus, indicating the detachment from materialistic emotions

A little deeper into the story of Maa Shailputri/Parvati, we can learn

- The act of taking the first step and embracing the discomfort of incompetence

- The nature of the mother as a resolute mountain

- Detaching from the noise of the mind and worldly desires

But on a spiritual and psychological level, we can learn the following:

- Recognize the fears, doubts, anger, and all emotions

- Embrace the discomfort of holding those emotions

- Taking actions despite those emotions

## Conclusion

So, from the first day of Navratri, we can conclude that in modern times, the sheer act of starting is enough for achieving any of your goals. People, your mind, will scream at you that it is impossible, it is worthless, it is not for you, it won’t matter, just listen and let it creep the discomfort for a moment, stay calm but headstrong in your resolve. Don’t mould your decisions without actions, don’t let analysis paralysis creep inside you. When the world around you seems to cloud your judgement, hold the hand of Maa Shailputri, let her divine penance be an example, let her show you the way of action, let her mould our destiny of ours.
