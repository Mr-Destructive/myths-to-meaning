It is day six, and we have gone through a considerable transformation in the past 5 days. Accepting the discomfort of acting, embracing your past deeds, devoting yourself to the situation, fostering kindness, and detaching from desires. Now, on day six, it is about defeating your inner demon. The desire, deep-rooted desires, and evil intentions, if any.

Day six of Navratri embraces the most powerful, or I should say the most fierce form of Maa Durga, Maa Katyayani, the slayer of Mahishasura.

## Maa Katyayani

Once, there was a demon, Mahishasura was strong and cruel. The gods approached Brahma, Vishnu, and Shiva. Out of their joint energy emerged a divine power to fight evil. That energy was shaped in the grounds of sage Katyayana. Because the sage worshipped her first, she is called Katyayani (daughter of Katyayana).

Armed with weapons given by the gods, she rode a lion and confronted Mahishasura. He asked for her hand in marriage. She replied that he must win her by battle. Mahishasura transformed into a buffalo. She leapt on him, struck his head with her foot, then severed it with her sword. Thus, she became known as Mahishasuramardini, the slayer of Mahishasura.

Maa Katyayani is the fierce divine force born to destroy inner and outer evil, to uphold dharma (righteousness), to protect the world.

## Acknowledging a demon exists within

Mahishasura is not only an external demon. He also stands for the inner evil, the destructive desires, anger, and ego. Even though the gods created her externally, the fight is internal.

We must accept that within us are negative impulses, envy, greed, fear, and anger. Only by seeing them clearly can we begin transformation.

Practise honest self-reflection. Use journaling to name your inner negative patterns. Be gentle but firm. Through mindfulness, observe when these “inner demons” arise without judging them, and do not act on them.

I use a daily journal to record my thoughts. I noticed, I had feelings of guilt and fear of failure before even taking action. This trend was noticed because the words that came out on paper or on a digital screen, those were visible. All those words were running in the brain the whole time, only when we spit them out, we realise the demons and angels within us. Journaling or writing out loud is just one method that has workedrked for me, it might be talking to someone or just any form of art that you long to do, just express yourself, without a purpose. In the modern world, productivity is causing a lot of hobbies to dwindle and cause stress and anxiety due to the lost connection of art.

## Fostering solitude to find the cause

The goddess emerges in the sage’s hermitage, where devotion, penance, solitude shape her energy. Before the battle, there is inner preparation.

To confront negativity in us, we need quiet time, solitude, reflection. In silence we hear deeper truths. Allocate daily quiet time. Even 10 minutes of stillness, or walking alone, meditating. In solitude, ask: “Why did I react that way?” “What is the root of this desire?”

Just as I said, having a small moment to reflection, just allow your mind to calm, in the modern world, the digital media is not letting us be bored. Embrace boredom, embrace soltitude. Boredom is good for your brain, or rather required in certain quantity for it to be at its best.

Allow youself to be vulnerable with your own soul, with whom are you escaping from? With whom are you hiding yourself from? Till what extent would that be hidden? Eventually you’ll have to admit yourself, the faster you do, the better you’ll feel the rest of your life.

## Practising self-control

The goddess does not immediately strike, she gives Mahishasura a chance, asks that he win her by fighting. She chooses timing, strategy, restraint.

We cannot always act rashly or suppress all impulses. We must choose what to respond to, when. Self-control means not being a slave to every urge.

Use small practices, pause before speaking in anger, count to ten, breathe deeply. Use discipline, decide your limits (food, screen time, words).

I cannot stress the direction of anger enough, words spoken out cannot be taken back, the only thing that gets inside after lashing out with anger is guilt and regret. Restrain from impulsive behaviour, just calm your mind down. I know it is hard to think, but it has always hurt if you think back now.

> “If you had been given two choices of Empathy and Anger, always choose empathy”

Always choose empathy if the slightest of judgment is available to your mind.

## Meaning from the Myths

Maa Katyayani, the fierce goddess born from the energy of the gods, shows us that the real battle is both outside and inside. She destroyed Mahishasura, who also represents anger, ego, and hidden desires within us.

The first step is to admit these inner demons exist. Through journaling, talking, or creative expression, we can see our thoughts clearly and begin to transform them.

Solitude is important. Just as the goddess emerged in a hermitage, we too must give ourselves quiet time to understand why we feel or act a certain way. Allow boredom, reflect, and be honest with yourself.

Self-control is the final lesson. Maa Katyayani chose the right moment to fight, teaching us to pause before reacting. Restraining anger and choosing empathy helps us avoid regret and brings peace.

The myth reminds us that strength is not only in fighting but also in preparation, reflection, and using power with wisdom. The fight is not always physical, its about being aware and strategizing actively to avoid one.

May Maa Katyayani bless you with the divine power to conquer over your inner demons!
