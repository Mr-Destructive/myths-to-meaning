We have reached half way through the 9 days of Navratri, there were 4 nights before this, and including today there will be 5 more nights. So, lets explore the fifth form of Maa Durga.

This is a turning point. The energy starts to shift from the early impulse for devotion to deeper reflection. And today, we meet a very special and divine form of Maa Durga, Maa Skandamata.

She is the mother of Lord Skanda, also known as Kartikeya, the god of war. But she is not just a mother in the usual way, no mother is. She is a mother with divine power, with patience, with quiet strength, you might say that’s every mother but she is an example of being one in the first place. Her story is not only about protection and love, but also about letting go and helping others walk their own path.

## Maa Skandamata

Long ago, the gods were facing a terrible threat. A powerful demon named Tarakasura had become almost impossible to defeat. He had received a boon that only the son of Lord Shiva could kill him. At the time, Shiva was deep in meditation, lost in penance after the death of his first wife, Sati. He had turned away from the world completely. But the world needed him again. After many years, Shiva finally accepted Maa Parvati, and they were united.

From this divine union, a powerful energy was born, Skanda, also known as Kartikeya, Murugan, or Subrahmanya, depending on the region.

But this was no ordinary birth. Kartikeya was born not in a usual way, but from the divine spark or seed of Shiva, which was so powerful that it could not be handled by any ordinary being. According to different versions of the story, this spark was carried by Agni, the god of fire, and later cooled in the sacred Ganga river. Eventually, Kartikeya was born from this divine energy and raised for a while by the Krittikas, six celestial mothers, in the Saravana forest, which is why he is also called Saravanabhava.

Once he was ready, Kartikeya was taken back to Parvati, who took him into her arms with love and pride. She accepted him completely, raised him, and gave him strength. Though his birth was surrounded by fire, war, and divine plans, she gave him comfort, care, and direction.

And so she became Skandamata,

> Skanda means Kartikeya, Mata means mother,

She is the mother of Skanda, the divine commander of the army of the gods.

She raised a child who was born to fight demons, but she did it with love. She did not make him soft, nor did she fill him with hate. She gave him balance, the calm of a lotus and the roar of a lion. That is why she is always shown with Kartikeya sitting in her lap, even while she rides a lion, holding a lotus in her hand.

This image is very meaningful. She is calm, but powerful. She is soft, but fearless. She holds her son close, but she also gives him the strength to leave her and fight for what is right. That is the balance of love and strength.

She reminds us that being a mother, or a caregiver of any kind, is not only about keeping someone safe. It is also about helping them become strong, even if that means they must face the world alone.

## Accepting your past deeds

Maa Skandamata shows us that we cannot run away from our past. Even gods had to face the results of their actions. Lord Shiva went into deep meditation after losing Sati. He closed himself from the world. But still, life came back to him. The world needed him again. He had to open his heart, accept Parvati, and create a new path.

In our life also, we make mistakes, we lose people, we go through pain, lose jobs, fail in exams, get overwhelmed with actions. But we cannot hide forever. There comes a time when we have to stand up, take responsibility, and continue our journey. Maa Skandamata reminds us that new beginnings can happen, but only when we accept what has already happened.

She did not question the birth of Kartikeya, or the unusual way he came into her life. She accepted him fully, without doubt, without condition. That is the power of acceptance. It gives peace. It gives clarity.

## Selfless Care and Love

When we talk about Skandamata, we talk about love, but it is not a selfish love. It is a love without pride, without asking anything in return.

She raised Kartikeya not for her own happiness, but for the good of the world. She gave him care, protection, and wisdom, but not for show, not to take credit. She did it because it was right.

Even in today’s time, we see people who help others quietly, who support without big words or expectations. That is the spirit of Skandamata. Simple, silent, and strong.

Her love teaches us how to serve others without losing our own strength. To give from the heart, but not become weak or dependent. To be soft, but not break.

We learned that kindness is not a weakness from the last day as the lesson from Maa Kushmanda. Today, Maa Skandmata amplifies it even further by giving kindness a selfless form.

## Detaching from desires

Skandamata is shown sitting with her child, but also riding a lion. She is not just holding him with attachment. She is also ready to let him go when it is time.

This is very difficult. Many times, we love someone so much that we want to keep them close always. But real love is not about control. It is about giving space, its about trust, not attachment or ego. About letting the other person grow, even if it takes them away from you.

Maa Skandamata raised Kartikeya with full love. But when the time came, she allowed him to go and fight Tarakasura. She did not stop him. She did not cry and beg him to stay. She trusted him. She knew her job was to prepare him, not to keep him forever.

This is the lesson of detachment. To do your duty with full heart, and then let go. Without fear. Without holding back.

## Meaning from the Myths

The story of Maa Skandamata might be a story from old times. But it is something we all live in different ways.

We are all sometimes like Shiva, closing off after pain. We are sometimes like Parvati, waiting and hoping for love. And we can also be like Skandamata, giving support, showing care, and preparing someone for their bigger purpose.

Navratri is a time to reflect on these qualities. To look inside and ask, am I accepting my past, or running from it? Am I loving people with attachment, or with trust? Am I giving to others selflessly, or expecting something back?

If we can bring even a little bit of Skandamata’s wisdom in our daily life, we will become stronger, more peaceful, and more balanced.

I have experienced pain, too all of us do. But we can’t cry and sit because someone did something in the past and that resulted in your bad times. No, that is just delaying your own actions. You are attaching yourself to your action, and would take the fruit if it only was good, not if that turned to be a bad one.

Remember to only act, result is not in your control. Be selfless, act but don’t attach yourself too much. Devote yourself? yes, but not bind your expectation in the result.

May Maa Parvati bless you with kindness and good results.
