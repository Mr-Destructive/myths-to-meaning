The more we dedicate our time and effort into something, the more we start to develop a affection or “this is my thing”, kind of an attachment. Just like kids, when we were small, and parents or anyone gave us toys we used to think of it as it is mine, now you realise it was just a medium to keep you happy and self-entangled (not saying our parents are heartless or something). We spend time, we start to take care of it, and it creates a kind of memories which we makes us hard to leave it aside. But we forget, that we are born in the world without anything and we go back without anything.

We can care about a thing, but the line between care and control gets blurred over time.

## Introduction

I had worked with my current employer for around 3 years now, and I kind of have gotten attached to it, its almost one of the products is like “my thing”, I maintain and own it. I have improved it over time, kept it consistent and ever-improving. But with that our mind starts to get influence with ego and it wants us to think that we are the sole owners of it. Which I don’t think so, we are. No one is. Responsibility and ownership yes, that is one thing, but thinking of it as a thing that you control is a bit scary, it gets really dangerous when the thing is humans. It happens with partners, friends, mother-children(over-affection can cause this too). Happens every where, if the person is deeply attached to him/her and finds quite challenging when he/her is not there with him/her.

## Care without Control

For living beings we should control anyone. When I see a pet, I feel partly good and sad. Good for that animal or bird who is feed regularly and loved, but in that its freedom is lost. We control them, we keep them caged and bounded. That feels a bit inhuman to me, I do love animals and birds but I don’t want to bind them to my will, that is opposite of love, its the infatuation for that being that you develop instead which hurts you and them both in the long run. If we keep the pets for our self-interest and needs, then that is wrong, but if its for their care and growth or protection then I think its kind of good? I get it its much better then living on the streets or in the wild, especially now in the modern populated world. But you might be restricting its path to freedom and bondage form this world, or maybe not.

So, I am not saying to not care for people or things, but don’t act like you are the master of that being.

## Control with Care

If you are in control of the being for some uncontrolled circumstances, then have care for it. If you are dealing with beings that are a bit stubborn and are assigned to be incharge of them as elder or guardian, then don’t be harsh, be compassionate. Care is something that can transform evil to goodness. I am not entirely sure or have seen this kind of control with care, like people just control without mercy. Some people have assumptions that they are the master of them.

## Conclusion

On the fifth day of Navratri, we worship Maa Skandmata, the mother with her son, Skanda, also known as Kartikeya. Her maternal form gives us a meaningful starting point to reflect on care, nurturing, and responsibility. You can read about the principles and values we can learn from Maa Skand Mata in the post that I wrote [last year](https://mythstomeaning.substack.com/p/navratri-day-5-selfless-love-and) or in [2024](https://mythstomeaning.substack.com/p/navratri-day-5-2024-maa-skandmata).

Nurture is the word I think, we are feeding someone, but the line between care and feeling like we are the lords of those being is dangerous. Care without attachment and don’t let ego take over your generous mind and soul.

Jay Bhavani ! Jay Ambe !
